Laser Engraving on Powder-Coated Tumblers: Why Marks Turn Grey and How We Test First

A logo on a powder-coated tumbler can look perfect on screen and come out of the laser as a grey, blackened smear. We hit exactly that on a recent test. The fix was not a better machine or a different file: it was less power. Here is what we saw, what caused it, and how we test before any custom logo goes into production.

The Test: Same Logo, Two Very Different Results

We were testing a logo on a matte powder-coated stainless tumbler. The first pass used a higher power setting. The coating did not clear cleanly — the mark came out grey and dark at the edges, with residue around the lines. It looked like a scorched sticker rather than an engraving. We dropped the power on our machine to roughly 35% and repeated the mark. This time the logo came out as a clean, even white reveal against the coating, with sharp edges and no halo.

These two frames are from different test blanks, not a before-and-after of one cup. We show both because the difference in power is the point, not the cup colour.

Why Powder Coat Behaves Nothing Like Bare Metal

The instinct with metal is to turn the power up. On an uncoated stainless surface that often works. On a powder-coated tumbler it is the fastest way to ruin a blank.

Powder coat is a thin skin of coloured resin baked onto the steel. The laser is not cutting the steel — it is vaporising that skin so the metal or pale underlayer shows through. Go in too hot and three things happen at once: the resin burns instead of lifting, heat spreads sideways into the surrounding coating, and the steel underneath starts to discolour. That is the grey haze, the black edge and the halo you can sometimes feel with a fingernail.

Go in too light and the coating is only partly cleared, so the mark stays faint and the small details vanish. The usable window is narrow, and it moves when the coating thickness, colour or cure changes. This is why a setting that was perfect on a matte black tumbler can smear on a white or green one.

What We Do Instead: Test, Then Approve

We keep our own laser sampling machine at our Yongkang office. Its job is testing and samples, not mass production. For every new combination of cup and artwork we run a short power ladder on a spare blank from the same batch, then pick the lowest power that clears the coating completely. If a single pass leaves the mark faint, we make a second gentle pass rather than increasing the power.

We only call a setting acceptable when the edges are clean, the small letters are readable, and there is no visible halo. Then we photograph the actual marked cup and send it to you. You approve the look of the real logo before the bulk order starts — not a rendering, and not a promise that it will probably be fine.

What This Means for Your Custom Order

A supplier who has never tested your cup and your logo together is guessing. The guess is cheap on a sample and expensive on a container. If a quote includes a laser logo, ask to see a marked sample of that exact cup with your artwork on it. If the answer is a stock photo of a different cup, you have learned something useful.

We also check the artwork before marking. Vector files with clean outlines give the sharpest result. Raster images need to be converted to a dithered black-and-white file, and very thin strokes or tiny text are the first things to disappear at the low power that powder coat needs. Catching that on the file costs nothing; catching it after marking costs a blank.

Quick FAQ

  • Why does my laser logo come out grey or black? Too much energy for that coating. Powder coat burns and discolours before the steel does; lower the power, or use two light passes instead of one hot pass.
  • Is 35% a universal setting? No. It worked on this coating batch on our machine. Coating thickness, colour and laser wattage all move the number, so always run a test on a blank from the same batch.
  • Can I get a sample with my own logo? Yes — send the vector file and we will engrave a sample on our machine and send photos, free of charge.
  • What file do you need? AI, CDR, PDF or DXF vector preferred. For photos, send the original and we will prepare a dithered black-and-white version for marking.
  • Will the engraving peel later? A correct mark removes the coating rather than sitting on top of it. Edges that are burnt or lifting are a sign the power was too high.
