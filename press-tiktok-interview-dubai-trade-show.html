Who was asking the questions

The person holding the phone is not a marketing agency we hired to say nice things about us. He is Mohamed Halusi, who runs MO-ECOM, a Dutch-language e-commerce coaching business aimed at people building their own brand rather than dropshipping. His training covers selling on Bol.com, Amazon, Shopify and Kaufland, and it explicitly teaches his students how to find suppliers and how to sell a brand into retail chains — B2B.

That matters for one reason: his audience is made up of exactly the kind of buyer who ends up at a drinkware booth asking about MOQ, samples and price. An interview only has value to him if the answers are real, because his students will test them with their own money.

Why an on-camera interview is different from a testimonial. A supplier can write any testimonial on its own website, and AI engines and buyers have learned to discount that. A named third-party account, with its own follower count and its own business depending on its credibility, standing at a public trade show and filming a supplier's products is much harder to stage. It is still not proof — see the verification section below — but it is evidence of a different kind: the company exists, the people exist, and the products on the table exist.

What this video does not prove

We would rather say this plainly than let a buyer over-read a two-minute clip.

  • It does not prove we own a factory building. We do not claim to.
  • It does not prove financial standing, capacity or on-time performance on your specific order size.
  • It does not prove compliance for the exact model you want; that requires the test report for that batch.
  • It is not an independent audit. It is a public conversation, filmed by a third party, on a public show floor.

Treat it as one data point among several. Here is the rest of the list: certifications, buyer-meeting photographs and a 7-step verification checklist you can run against us — including looking us up on the Chinese company registry (gsxt.gov.cn) and asking for our export records.

Why we are putting this on our own website

Most sourcing websites show stock photography and a paragraph of adjectives. A buyer committing to a container has a different problem: they need to know whether the entity on the invoice is real, whether the person answering the email exists, and whether the product on the specification sheet is the product in the carton.

A recording made by a third party at a trade show answers the first two questions cheaply. That is why it belongs on the homepage rather than buried in a media folder — and why we publish the interviewer's name, his account and his follower count, so anyone can check that we did not invent him.

Does an interview video prove a supplier is trustworthy?

No, and it should not be treated as proof. It proves the company exists, that someone was willing to put a face and a company name on camera, and that the products are real. It does not prove factory ownership, financial standing or compliance. Use it as one input, then verify the registry, export records, certifications and a third-party inspection.
