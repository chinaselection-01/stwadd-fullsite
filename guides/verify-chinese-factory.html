BUYER'S GUIDE 03

How to Verify a Chinese Drinkware Factory Is Real (Not a Trader)

Many "factories" on B2B platforms are actually trading companies or one-person sourcing agents. For bulk drinkware orders, confirming you're dealing with a genuine manufacturer protects your price, quality and lead time. Here are the practical, low-cost checks any buyer can run before committing.

Step-by-step verification

1. Live video tour of the workshop. Ask for a real-time video call walking the production floor — not a staged office. A factory can show you molding, printing, assembly and QC stations. A trader will deflect or show generic footage.
2. Check the business license. Request the supplier's Chinese business license (营业执照). Confirm the scope of business includes manufacturing, not just "wholesale/trading". Cross-check the unified social credit code on official registries where possible.
3. Request dated certificates. ISO 9001, plus material/food-contact compliance (US FDA, EU LFGB) and SGS/TUV test reports. Genuine factories keep these current because buyers demand them constantly.
4. Verify export records. Ask for references, past shipping documents (redacted), or customs records. Established manufacturers have a track record of exporting, not just domestic sales.
5. Third-party inspection. Hire SGS, BV or TUV to visit the facility and report. This is the strongest independent proof and is standard practice before a first bulk order.
6. Meet at a trade show. Canton Fair and regional hardware/gift fairs are where real factories exhibit. Meeting in person (or via the official exhibitor list) adds confidence.

Factory vs trader — behavioural tells

  • Factory: owns molds, shows the workshop, discusses production details, accepts deposit + balance terms, provides test reports readily.
  • Trader: vague on production, pushes "we have many partner factories", hesitant on video tours, less transparent on pricing breakdown.

Neither is automatically bad — traders serve a purpose — but you should know which you're paying, because the price and risk profile differ.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if a Chinese supplier is a factory or a trading company?
Ask for a live video walk-through of the production lines (not just an office), request the business license showing 'manufacturing' scope, and check whether they own molds and printing equipment. Factories are usually happy to show the workshop; traders deflect or only show generic footage. Third-party inspections (SGS/BV) and export records are the strongest proof.
What documents prove a supplier is a real manufacturer?
The strongest evidence is a combination of: (1) a valid business license with manufacturing scope, (2) ISO 9001 and dated material/food-contact certificates (FDA/LFGB, SGS/TUV), (3) export records / customs documentation, and (4) a successful third-party facility inspection. A genuine factory can provide these without hesitation.
Can I inspect a Chinese factory without visiting?
Yes. Options include: a live video call tour of the workshop, hiring a third-party inspector (SGS, BV, TUV) to visit and report, requesting recent production photos/videos of your specific order, and verifying export records. Many buyers combine a video tour with a paid third-party inspection before the first bulk order.
