The HS code on your commercial invoice decides the duty you pay, the documents your broker needs, and whether your container sits at the port while someone argues about what a "vacuum flask" actually is. For drinkware the codes are few but easy to get wrong, because a steel vacuum bottle is not classified as steel. Below is the reference importers actually use, the rule that decides the code, country-specific notes, and a declaration clause you can paste into the invoice.

The most common error. Declaring a double-wall vacuum bottle under 7323.93 (steel) or 3924.10 (plastic) because the shell happens to be steel or plastic. It is the vacuum function that controls the heading — see the rule below.

02 Why a Vacuum Bottle Goes to 9617, Not to the Steel Heading

Under the General Rules of Interpretation (GRI 1), the wording of the heading wins before the material does. HS 9617 reads "Vacuum flasks and other vacuum vessels, complete with cases", and it names vacuum flasks expressly. That means a stainless steel vacuum bottle is classified under 9617.00 even though its shell is steel — and it beats heading 7323 (articles of stainless steel). The same logic applies to a plastic-bodied vacuum bottle: it still goes to 9617.00, not 3924.

Why this lands on you. A supplier who declares your vacuum bottle under 7323.93 or 3924.10 may be steering toward a lower duty rate. That can save a few points at the border, but if the classification is wrong, the risk lands on the importer of record — you — not the factory. The code should describe the product honestly; the duty is then what it is.

03 Country-Specific Notes (verify live before you quote)

The six-digit HS is shared, but every destination layers its own subheadings, duty and trade measures on top. Treat the following as direction, not a rate sheet — tariffs move, and the live subheading is what your broker files:

  • United States: vacuum bottles fall under HTS 9617.00.xx (for example 9617.00.60 for certain vacuum bottles). China-origin drinkware may also carry Section 301 additional duties on top of the base MFN rate; check the current HTS subheading and any exclusion before you commit a landed-cost number.
  • European Union: TARIC 9617 00 00 applies to vacuum flasks and is generally duty-free into the EU; single-wall steel tableware under 7323.93 carries a low ad valorem rate. Verify the live TARIC, and remember VAT is charged on top regardless of the duty rate.
  • United Kingdom: the UK tariff follows the same 9617 00 00 / 7323 93 structure, with vacuum flasks typically at 0% and a small rate on single-wall steel.
  • Australia: 9617 is concessionally treated for China origin under ChAFTA; confirm the current duty for your specific subheading.
  • Gulf / Middle East: GCC members apply the GCC tariff; rates and documentation differ by emirate and by whether the product is dual-use. Confirm per destination.

The pattern is the same everywhere: get the six-digit HS right first (vacuum = 9617, single-wall steel = 7323.93), then confirm the destination's subheading and rate.

04 The Declaration Clause for Your Invoice

Paste this onto the commercial invoice so the broker classifies without guessing. A vague "bottle" is the number-one cause of a hold at the port:

Commercial-invoice clause

Goods description: [Quantity] pcs of [single-wall / double-wall vacuum-insulated] [stainless steel / plastic / aluminium] [bottle / tumbler / flask], capacity [ml]. HS code: [9617.00 / 7323.93 / 3924.10 / 7615.19]. Country of origin: China. Declared value basis: FOB [port]. Material composition: [304 stainless steel body, PP lid / etc.]. Intended use: [reusable drinkware].

Match the description to the code. If the description says "vacuum-insulated" but the code is 7323.93, the broker will stop and ask — and that stop is your delay.

Frequently Asked Questions

What HS code do I use for a vacuum-insulated stainless steel bottle?
A double-wall vacuum-insulated bottle is classified under HS 9617.00 (vacuum flasks and other vacuum vessels, complete with cases) regardless of whether the shell is steel, plastic or a mix. In China export declarations it appears as 9617009000. The vacuum function, not the shell material, decides the heading.
Why is my supplier putting a single-wall code on a vacuum bottle?
Under the rules of classification, HS 9617 names vacuum flasks expressly and takes priority over the material headings such as 7323 (steel) or 3924 (plastic). If a vacuum bottle is declared under 7323.93 or 3924.10, check why: it may be a single-wall product mislabelled, or a supplier steering to a lower-duty code. The correct code protects you at clearance, not just the supplier.
What is the China export code for a stainless steel water bottle?
For a single-wall stainless steel bottle or cup, China uses 7323930000 (heading 7323.93). For a vacuum-insulated bottle, China uses 9617009000 (heading 9617.00). The two are different products with different duty treatment at destination, so the export code and the import HS must match the actual construction.
Do I need a different code for the US versus the EU?
The six-digit HS is the same (9617.00, 7323.93, 3924.10, 7615.19), but the destination adds its own subheadings and duty. The US uses an HTS ten-digit code (vacuum bottles fall under 9617.00.xx), the EU uses TARIC, and each applies its own rate and any trade measures. Confirm the live subheading for the specific destination before you quote landed cost.
What should I write on the commercial invoice to avoid clearance delays?
State the material (stainless steel / plastic / aluminium), capacity in ml, single or double wall, the HS code, country of origin (China), and the declared value basis (FOB). A clear description that matches the code lets the broker classify without guessing, which is the single biggest cause of hold-ups.
