8 Common Drinkware Quality Problems and How to Fix Them

When a container of bottles arrives with weak insulation, a faint logo, or cartons marked for the wrong customer, the last thing a buyer wants is a supplier who says "that is normal". These problems are common in drinkware, but they are also predictable — and most of them can be caught by a test or a check before the goods ship. This guide covers the eight we are asked about most, in plain language, with the checks that actually find them.

The eight problems covered here:
  1. Bottle not keeping drinks hot or cold (weak vacuum)
  2. Laser-engraved logo too light, grey or patchy
  3. UV print peeling, tacky or dull
  4. Printed logo blurry or grainy
  5. Messy packaging
  6. Wrong product or wrong box packed
  7. Wrong or unscannable barcode
  8. Wrong shipping mark on cartons

1Why is my insulated bottle not keeping drinks hot or cold?

SYMPTOM

Water cools in an hour or two. The outside of the cup feels warm when it is full of hot liquid, or the base sweats with cold drinks. The bottle looks perfect, so nobody suspects the vacuum.

WHY IT HAPPENS

A vacuum bottle is two steel walls with the air pumped out between them. Heat barely crosses a vacuum. If the sealed tail leaks, the weld is poor, or the getter that absorbs leftover gas fails, air creeps back in and the bottle becomes a single-wall cup. A knock in transit can open a microscopic leak. There is no visual check for this — which is exactly why weak vacuum is a recurring dispute.

HOW TO CHECK

Fill to the rated capacity with water at 95°C and measure at set intervals in a room around 20°C, using the real retail lid, not a test plug. Write the method and the numbers into the purchase order. Two widely used benchmarks: GB/T 29606-2013 — 6 hours at or above 68°C and 24 hours at or above 42°C; and QB/T 2332-2017 — 6 hours at or above 65°C and 12 hours at or above 50°C. A seller who will not name the test method has a slogan, not a specification.

Prevention: agree the retention numbers and test conditions before production, sample from several cartons, and re-check at pre-shipment. Two cups from the same box can differ by a factor of two if the sealing was unstable that day.

2Why does the laser-engraved logo look grey, dim or patchy?

SYMPTOM

The mark comes out grey instead of a clean white reveal, has a scorched halo, or the small letters vanish. Sometimes it looks fine on black and bad on green or white.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Powder coat is a thin skin of coloured resin over steel. The laser is meant to vaporise that skin cleanly so the pale layer below shows through. Too much power burns the resin and discolours the metal; too little only partly clears it. Coating thickness, colour and cure all shift the correct setting, and so does the lens and wattage of the machine.

HOW TO CHECK

Ask for a marked sample of the exact cup shape and coating colour with your artwork on it — not a stock photo of a different cup. Check under normal light: edges should be sharp, small text readable, and there should be no haze or ring you can feel with a fingernail.

Prevention: send a clean vector file and approve the real marked sample before the run. We keep our own laser sampling machine at the Yongkang office for exactly this, and mark samples free so you can see the result first.

3Why is the UV print peeling, tacky or dull?

SYMPTOM

The print feels sticky, rubs off on a finger, cracks at the edges, or the colour looks flatter than the approved sample.

WHY IT HAPPENS

UV ink is cured by light the moment it is laid down. If the ink is not matched to a powder-coated or stainless surface, the curing lamp is weak or too fast, or the cup carries oil and dust from handling, the ink sits on top instead of bonding. Artwork in the wrong colour mode or under a clear coat that is not compatible causes the rest.

HOW TO CHECK

On a pre-production sample, do a cross-hatch tape adhesion test and a wet rub test. Check for tackiness and compare the colour under the same light as the golden sample. These two tests take minutes and expose most adhesion problems before a full order.

Prevention: confirm the ink and surface pair, require a printed sample for sign-off, and put the adhesion test into the inspection checklist rather than relying on a photo.

4Why does our printed logo come out blurry or grainy?

SYMPTOM

Edges look soft, gradients break into visible dots, thin lines disappear, or small text is unreadable at normal viewing distance.

WHY IT HAPPENS

The usual cause is the artwork, not the printer: a logo pulled from a website, an image upscaled beyond its resolution, or a raster file printed larger than it was designed for. A coarse print dot and a textured coating add visible grain. On curved bottles, flat artwork that ignores the wrap also stretches the design.

HOW TO CHECK

Look at a physical sample at arm's length under normal shop light, not zoomed in on a screen. Supply vector files where possible, or 300 dpi at final print size, and keep strokes and lettering generous for the material.

Prevention: approve a printed sample of the actual cup before production. If the logo is too fine for the decoration method, a good supplier says so up front and suggests a fix instead of shipping it and hoping.

5What about messy or untidy packaging?

SYMPTOM

Inner boxes crushed or misaligned, protective film missing, lids scratched from rubbing together, cartons loosely packed with voids, or a general "thrown together" look that makes a retail buyer reject the delivery.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Messy packing is nearly always a missing written spec: no agreed carton count, no rule on one SKU per carton, no approved example of how the retail box should look and face. Without a golden sample, every packer improvises.

HOW TO CHECK

State the packing method in the order — units per inner box, inner boxes per carton, protective film, orientation — and attach a photo of the approved pack. Open random cartons at inspection and compare.

Prevention: a written packing spec and a golden sample cost nothing to agree and remove most of the argument later. For retail-ready goods, treat the carton as part of the product.

6What if the wrong product or the wrong box was packed?

SYMPTOM

A carton labelled for one SKU contains another. Mixed colours or capacities in one box. The customer receives someone else's artwork.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Similar-looking bottles packed side by side, no scanning at the packing station, and a rush at the end of the line. Hand-counting lookalike SKUs fails sooner or later, especially when several orders share the same cup body with different logos.

HOW TO CHECK

Require one SKU per carton, a clearly labelled packing area for each order, and a carton-level barcode scan before sealing. At inspection, open cartons across the whole batch — not just the top layer — and verify SKU, colour, capacity and artwork against the packing list.

Prevention: make SKU accuracy a listed defect category in the inspection criteria and a condition of final payment. If several suppliers' goods are involved, a buying office that consolidates under one checked packing list removes a whole layer of this risk.

7Why was the barcode wrong or unscannable?

SYMPTOM

The label shows another product's code, the scanner will not read it, or the retail system rejects it at goods-in.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Barcodes get copied from an older SKU, generated from an internal code instead of the buyer's GTIN, printed too small or too low-contrast, or simply stuck on the wrong carton. An unreadable barcode is functionally the same as a wrong one at a distribution centre.

HOW TO CHECK

Send the correct GTIN/EAN in writing and ask for a label proof before printing. At inspection, scan a sample of labels with a real scanner or phone app — never judge by eye — and confirm the number matches the invoice and the product.

Prevention: one written barcode list per order, a label proof approved before printing, and scanning built into the inspection step. This is a small check that prevents an expensive retail rejection.

8What if the shipping mark on the cartons is wrong?

SYMPTOM

Wrong consignee, wrong port, wrong carton numbers, a misspelled customer name, or marks that do not match the documents.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Shipping marks are often typed by hand from an old order, and nobody cross-checks them against the current invoice, packing list and bill of lading. A single character out can send cargo to the wrong consignee or hold it at customs.

HOW TO CHECK

Approve the mark artwork in writing before printing. Then compare it character for character with the commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading — consignee, destination, order or carton reference, gross and net weight. Photograph the marked cartons at loading.

Prevention: treat the shipping mark as a document, not a label. One person approves the text, and the same text appears on all three documents before the truck leaves.

The Common Thread: Write It Down, Then Inspect It

Almost every problem above comes from the same gap — something was agreed verbally, or not agreed at all. The fix is not a supplier with better intentions. It is a written spec, an approved sample, and an independent pre-shipment inspection covering thermal performance, decoration, packing, barcodes and carton marks, with the balance payment tied to the report.

Quick FAQ

  • Bottle not holding temperature? Test retention against a written standard — GB/T 29606-2013 or QB/T 2332-2017 — with the real retail lid, not a test plug.
  • Laser mark grey or dim? Wrong power for that coating, or a mark that was never sampled on your exact cup. Approve a real marked sample first.
  • UV print peeling? Ink-to-surface mismatch, weak curing, or oil on the cup. A cross-hatch and wet rub test on a sample catches it.
  • Blurry logo? Low-resolution artwork or a coarse print dot. Use vector or 300 dpi and approve a physical sample.
  • Wrong carton contents? One SKU per carton plus a carton-level scan before sealing, verified by inspection.
  • Barcode not scanning? Send the correct GTIN in writing, approve a label proof, and scan labels at inspection — never by eye.
  • Shipping mark wrong? Match it character for character with the invoice, packing list and bill of lading, and photograph the cartons at loading.
